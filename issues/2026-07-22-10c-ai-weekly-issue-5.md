## Headline of the week

European enterprises face a mounting regulatory challenge as the EU moves to enforce tech sovereignty and AI compliance. The European Commission’s “tech sovereignty” package, published on 3 June 2026, includes the Cloud and AI Development Act and cyber resilience laws aimed at reducing external dependencies ([Global Policy Watch, 18 July 2026](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/07/looking-beyond-the-tech-sovereignty-package-how-the-eu-is-moving-to-ensure-tech-sector-resilience/)). At the same time, a diginomica survey found that only a quarter of enterprises believe they are in scope of the EU AI Act, and 36% have not carried out an applicability assessment ([diginomica, 20 July 2026](https://diginomica.com/enterprise-hits-and-misses-cios-respond-looming-eu-ai-act-while-enterprises-break-away-frontier)).

**Looking beyond the tech sovereignty package: how the EU is moving to ensure tech sector resilience**  
*Analysis of the EU’s Cloud and AI Development Act and related laws, with implications for how enterprises source cloud and AI infrastructure. Published by Global Policy Watch, 18 July 2026.*

10C take: The new EU Cloud and AI Development Act will force many clients to reassess their cloud and AI vendor relationships, especially if they rely on non-European providers. Consultants should prepare to advise on compliance roadmaps and sovereignty requirements, and to help clients identify alternative European infrastructure options.

**Enterprise hits and misses – CIOs respond to the looming EU AI Act, while enterprises break away from frontier model addiction – but there are caveats**  
*Survey data showing widespread underpreparedness for the EU AI Act, with only 25% of enterprises believing they are in scope. Published by diginomica, 20 July 2026.*

10C take: The readiness gap represents a direct advisory opportunity. Many clients will need help conducting applicability assessments and building compliance frameworks before enforcement deadlines. This is especially urgent for financial services and healthcare clients where AI use cases are common and regulatory risk is high.

## The Briefs

**Models and tools**  
Chinese startup Moonshot AI released the Kimi K3 model, which the company says closes the gap with leading US systems, though it still trails Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5 and OpenAI’s GPT 5.6 Sol on overall performance ([CNBC, 17 July 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/17/moonshot-ai-kimi-k3-model-openai-anthropic-china.html)). Why it matters to us: European enterprises evaluating model sourcing need to monitor Chinese models as viable alternatives for cost or sovereignty reasons; this may shift procurement decisions and risk assessments.

**Proof point**  
WeRide’s autonomous driving deployment in the United Arab Emirates was selected as one of ten case studies in a national report on Chinese AI international cooperation, presented at the 2026 World AI Conference. It was the only autonomous driving company recognized ([markets.businessinsider.com/Globe Newswire, 18 July 2026](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/weride-named-among-china-s-top-10-ai-case-studies-for-uae-autonomous-driving-deployment-the-only-autonomous-driving-company-recognized-1036338295)). Why it matters to us: Demonstrates that real-world autonomous logistics deployments are happening beyond pilot stages; relevant for clients in European logistics and transportation evaluating autonomous fleet investments.

**Enterprise and industry**  
Capgemini and Volvo executives discussed how AI, digital twins, and smarter supply chains are being applied in automotive manufacturing to improve efficiency and resilience ([Automotive News, 15 July 2026](https://www.autonews.com/sponsored/in-the-drivers-seat/ane-20260715-rss-capgemini-volvo-leaders-on-how-ai-smarter-supply-chains-next-gen-factories/)). Why it matters to us: Provides a concrete, branded example of AI-driven manufacturing transformation that can be cited in client conversations about supply chain digitisation and Industry 4.0 roadmaps.

**Proof point**  
FinHarbor launched an AI Act-ready AML compliance module that runs a self-hosted LLM inside client infrastructure and keeps every decision with regulatory consequences in human hands ([markets.businessinsider.com/Globe Newswire, 20 July 2026](https://markets.businessinsider.com/news/stocks/finharbor-launches-ai-co-investigator-for-aml-1036340423)). Why it matters to us: Shows a financial services product explicitly designed for EU AI Act compliance (self-hosted, human-in-the-loop); relevant for banking and insurance clients seeking compliant AI deployment in regulated workflows.

**Enterprise and industry**  
According to PitchBook’s Q2 2026 European Venture Report, H1 2026 European venture deal value reached €44 billion, with AI startups capturing 60% (€26.5 billion); mega-rounds accounted for 44% of deal value ([PitchBook, 17 July 2026](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/european-vc-funding-narrows-around-a-handful-of-ai-bets)). Why it matters to us: The concentration of funding into a few large AI bets signals a market that is both dynamic and risky; clients should consider strategic partnerships, build-vs-buy analysis, and the implications of a tightening capital environment for suppliers.

**Proof point**  
CompTIA’s inaugural “AI Skills Tracker” found that 57% of workers use AI tools regularly but only 28% consider themselves advanced in AI, and AI learning remains largely informal ([PR Newswire/CompTIA, 21 July 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/survey-of-business-and-tech-leaders-reveals-persistent-gap-between-corporate-ai-adoption-and-workforce-readiness-302830296.html)). Why it matters to us: Quantifies the workforce readiness challenge that our clients face; consultants can sell training, change management, and operating model redesign services to close the gap between deployment and outcomes.

**Proof point**  
The KLAS Global HIT Trends 2026 Report revealed that artificial intelligence has become the number-one healthcare IT investment priority across every global region tracked outside the United States, with 37% of organizations prioritizing AI strategy and governance ([HIT Consultant, 16 July 2026](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/07/16/klas-global-healthcare-it-trends-2026/)). Why it matters to us: Direct evidence that healthcare clients across Europe are making AI their top IT priority; this creates immediate demand for strategic advisory on AI roadmaps, governance, and vendor selection in the healthcare vertical.

## Client lens

This week’s developments reinforce two core themes for our advisory work. First, the EU’s tech sovereignty push and the AI Act readiness gap create a clear compliance advisory opportunity across all sectors, especially for financial services and healthcare clients who face the highest regulatory scrutiny. Second, the workforce readiness gap highlighted by CompTIA and the VC concentration data suggest that clients need to focus not just on AI adoption but on the organizational and talent changes required to make that adoption effective.

The Capgemini/Volvo case study and the FinHarbor AML tool provide concrete examples we can use to make abstract concepts like digital twins or AI Act compliance tangible for clients. The Moonshot AI model and WeRide deployment serve as reminders that the competitive landscape is shifting, and that European enterprises should not assume only US vendors will dominate their AI strategy.
