## Headline of the week

**Compliance deadline approaches as enterprise agent adoption stalls**

Two developments this week frame the tension in enterprise AI: new regulatory obligations and a sobering reality check on scaling from pilot to production.

**[Made by AI? EU tells firms to stick a label on it from Sunday](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/made-ai-eu-tells-firms-051905943.html)**
*From 2 August 2026, the EU AI Act’s transparency provisions require companies to label AI-generated content, including deepfakes and chatbot outputs, so users can clearly distinguish synthetic from human-created material. (Yahoo News, 28 July 2026)*

10C take: Clients deploying customer-facing chatbots, marketing content generators, or any AI system that interacts with EU end users must now integrate labelling into their output workflows. Consultants should assess existing audit trails, decide whether labelling happens at the model level or application layer, and clarify liability if labels are omitted. This is a concrete compliance item that cannot wait.

**[Cognizant Launches EMEA AI Unit as Enterprise Agent Pilots Fail at Scale](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321781/20260728/cognizant-launches-emea-ai-unit-enterprise-agent-pilots-fail-scale.htm)**
*Cognizant formed a dedicated EMEA AI Unit on 28 July 2026, citing IDC research that 88% of AI agent proofs-of-concept never reach production. (Tech Times, 28 July 2026)*

10C take: The 88% statistic is a powerful data point to use when clients ask why their own agent pilots are stalling. The barrier is rarely technology alone; it is often integration with existing systems, unclear ROI, or regulatory uncertainty. Cognizant’s investment signals that major system integrators see a market for services that bridge exactly this gap. Consultants can reference this to build realistic adoption roadmaps that identify pilot-to-production risks early.

## The Briefs

**Regulation and governance**

**EU hits Google with $1 billion fine over its Play app store and search.** The European Commission imposed the fine under digital market rules, marking another major antitrust action against a large tech platform. ([The Sun Chronicle (AP), 23 July 2026](https://www.thesunchronicle.com/business/eu-hits-google-with-1-billion-fine-over-its-play-app-store-and-search/article_1ec5eee1-05eb-5ddd-b266-313f8392d4ca.html))  
Why it matters to us: The fine reinforces that EU regulators are willing to levy significant penalties. Clients who rely on Google’s enterprise cloud and AI services should monitor whether Google adjusts pricing or service terms in response, and factor this into their vendor risk assessments.

**Models and tools**

**Anthropic releases new model, Opus 5.** On 24 July 2026, Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5, which delivers performance close to its flagship Fable 5 across many tasks while costing $5 per million input tokens – half the price of Fable 5. ([Axios, 24 July 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/24/anthropic-releases-new-model-opus-5))  
Why it matters to us: Enterprise clients evaluating model providers now have a strong lower-cost alternative for coding, knowledge work, and general tasks. Consultants can benchmark Opus 5 against GPT-5 and Gemini for cost-sensitive production deployments in Europe.

**Microsoft launches its first cybersecurity model, plus a new agentic cybersecurity system.** On 27 July 2026, Microsoft unveiled MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, a cybersecurity-specialised AI model, alongside an agentic platform designed to find and fix vulnerabilities in complex codebases. ([TechCrunch, 27 July 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/27/microsoft-launches-its-first-cyber-model-and-a-new-agentic-cybersecurity-system/))  
Why it matters to us: Financial services and healthcare clients with high security requirements gain a new tool for automated vulnerability discovery. Consultants can assess whether this proprietary model reduces reliance on third-party AI security tools and whether it fits existing Microsoft Enterprise Agreement licensing.

**Enterprise and industry**

**Ex-Accel investor raises $30 million to build industrial AI company Arrakis.** Arrakis, founded by ex-Accel investor Rafael Quintanilla and alumni of Palantir, ASML, and Datadog, raised $30 million in Series A funding to deploy AI agents for mission-critical operations in aerospace, energy, logistics, and manufacturing. ([businesscloud.co.uk, 22 July 2026](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/ex-accel-investor-raises-28m-to-build-industrial-ai-company/))  
Why it matters to us: Growing venture appetite for industrial AI agents targeting core operational workflows signals that heavy industry and logistics are becoming a focus for AI startups. Clients should watch whether Arrakis competes with or is acquired by existing OT/IT vendors.

**Google increases capex forecast again after cloud-driven quarterly beat.** Alphabet reported its best-ever quarter for cloud revenue growth on 22 July 2026, but raised capital expenditure plans by $15 billion, driven by continued delays to its flagship AI model and infrastructure investment needs. ([Reuters, 22 July 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/google-quarterly-cloud-revenue-growth-beats-expectations-2026-07-22/))  
Why it matters to us: Rising cloud capex across hyperscalers means enterprise clients should expect higher cloud costs but also more AI capabilities integrated into platform services. Consultants can help clients model the financial impact of shifting to cloud-native AI workloads versus on-premise deployments.

**Proof point**

**Chinese new energy enterprise achieves full-process collaborative intelligence.** Sigenergy’s Nantong Smart Energy Center in China connected every key piece of equipment to the industrial internet, moving from single-point automation to full-process collaborative intelligence in energy storage production. ([Xinhua News Agency, 26 July 2026](https://english.news.cn/20260726/e54baa7abc3e443c99b36386123257ed/c.html))  
Why it matters to us: This real-world case shows that transformation from isolated automation to interconnected AI-driven manufacturing is already happening in energy storage. European manufacturers in battery, renewable energy, and industrial equipment can use this as a benchmark for their own Industry 4.0 roadmaps.

## Client lens

Two themes this week demand immediate attention from our clients. First, the EU AI Act transparency deadline means any client deploying generative AI in customer-facing applications needs a labelling workflow by 2 August. This applies across manufacturing (marketing content), financial services (chatbots), healthcare (patient communications), and logistics (supplier correspondence). Second, the 88% pilot-to-production failure rate for AI agents is a concrete statistic that can anchor a conversation about realistic adoption planning. Clients in energy and heavy industry launching agent pilots should be asked: what is your plan to avoid becoming part of that 88%?

For financial services and healthcare clients, the Microsoft cybersecurity model offers a new option for vulnerability discovery that may be easier to procure through existing enterprise agreements. For clients with cost pressures, the Anthropic Opus 5 pricing provides a lever to renegotiate model contracts.
