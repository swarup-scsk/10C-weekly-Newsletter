## Client lens

The sectors 10C advises are the ones where agents act on real systems and real records: manufacturing and logistics operations, energy infrastructure, healthcare workflows, and financial services processes. In each of those settings, the question of what an agent is allowed to do, and what evidence exists that it stayed inside those limits, is closer to operational risk than to software selection. An announcement about safety boundaries from a major platform vendor is therefore relevant to risk, audit and compliance stakeholders, not only to the engineering team.

The engagement opportunity is in the gap between a reference design and a working control. Clients will need help deciding where agent boundaries sit, who approves changes to them, what gets logged, and how that evidence is produced when an auditor or regulator asks. That is consulting work that sits between architecture, risk and process design, and it usually starts with a short assessment rather than a large programme.

## Conversation Starter

A point you can raise in a client conversation this week:

> If we put an AI agent into this process next quarter, who signs off that it stays inside its boundaries once it is live, and what would we show an auditor to prove it?

This opens a useful conversation because it moves the discussion away from model choice and towards operational control, which is where most clients have not yet made a decision.
